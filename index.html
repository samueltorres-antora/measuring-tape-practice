How a ruler works
Understanding inches, halves, quarters, eighths & sixteenths
Foundation
1
Every inch is divided into 16 equal parts
The distance between two whole numbers (e.g. 0″ and 1″) is split into 16 tiny slices. Each slice is 1/16″. All other fractions are just groups of those slices.
2
Tick height = fraction importance
The ruler uses height as a shortcut — you don't need to count every tiny line. Taller ticks mark bigger fractions. Learn the 5 heights and you can read any position instantly.
Whole inch — tallest, printed number
Half inch — 2nd tallest, easy to spot
Quarter inch — medium height
Eighth inch — short
Sixteenth inch — shortest
3
Use landmarks, not counting
The fastest readers navigate like a map. Find the nearest big landmark (whole inch → half → quarter), then count tiny ticks from there. Example: to find 7/16″, find the ½″ mark and step left one tiny tick — that's it.

Reading fractions on a ruler
Step-by-step method for any measurement
Technique
1
Start at the nearest whole inch to the left
Look at the measurement point. Find the closest whole-inch number that is at or to the left of it. That's your base. Example: if a point is between 2″ and 3″, your base is 2″.
2
Find the nearest landmark between that inch and the point
Look for the tall ½″ tick first. Is the point before or after it? Then narrow to ¼ or ¾. Use the height of the nearest tick to place yourself quickly.
3
Count tiny (sixteenth) ticks from the landmark
Once you're oriented to the nearest quarter or half, count the short ticks left or right to reach the exact point. Each tiny tick = 1/16″.
Worked example — read this point
①Arrow is between 0″ and 1″ — base is 0″
②Arrow is past the ½ mark, before the ¾ mark — between ½ and ¾
③Count from ¾ going left: arrow is 1 tiny tick before ¾
✓Answer: 11/16″ (¾ minus 1/16)

Adding measurements
How to add two lengths on a tape measure
Prep for Quiz 2
1
Convert everything to sixteenths first
Fractions are easiest to add when they share a common unit. Since the smallest division is 1⁄16″, convert each measurement: 1″ = 16⁄16, ½″ = 8⁄16, ¼″ = 4⁄16, ⅛″ = 2⁄16, 1⁄16 = 1⁄16.
1″= 16 sixteenths
½″= 8 sixteenths
¼″= 4 sixteenths
⅛″= 2 sixteenths
1⁄16″= 1 sixteenth
2
Add the sixteenths, then simplify
Add the two sixteenth counts together, then convert back. If the total is 16 or more, divide by 16 to get whole inches. Simplify any leftover fraction if possible.
Worked example — ¾″ + ⅝″
①¾″ = 12 sixteenths
②⅝″ = 10 sixteenths
③12 + 10 = 22 sixteenths
④22 ÷ 16 = 1 remainder 6 → 1 and 6⁄16
✓Simplified: 1⅜″ (6⁄16 = ⅜)

Subtracting measurements
How to find what remains after cutting or trimming
Prep for Quiz 2
1
The same sixteenths trick works for subtraction
Convert both measurements to sixteenths, subtract, then convert back. This avoids any fraction confusion — it's just plain number subtraction.
2
Real-world use: cutting a piece to length
You have a board that measures 2¼″. You need to cut 11⁄16″ off one end. What's left? Convert both to sixteenths, subtract, convert back.
Worked example — 1½″ − 5⁄16″
①1½″ = 1×16 + 8 = 24 sixteenths
②5⁄16″ = 5 sixteenths
③24 − 5 = 19 sixteenths
④19 ÷ 16 = 1 remainder 3 → 1 and 3⁄16
✓Answer: 1 3⁄16″
